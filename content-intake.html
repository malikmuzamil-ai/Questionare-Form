One rule for what you give away. Do not invent anything. Look at what is already inside your offer, your audit, your call questions, one part of your method, and cut a piece off it. If you have to build it this month, pick something else.
1They have the problem but they have named it wrong
What stops them
Their words, out loud, even though they are wrong about it.
They say "I post consistently and nothing happens." They think it is reach. It is what they post about. Write the quote, not the diagnosis.
What fixes it
Three or four words.
They name it right. Or: they measure the right thing. Something they do, not something they understand.
What you hand them
Both in one line.
Your new client audit runs 30 checks. Cut it to six they can run alone and call it The 60-Second Post Audit. It tells them what is wrong. It does not tell them what to write instead, which is what they pay you for.
2They believe the problem but not that your answer is different
What stops them
They say "everyone says the same thing", or "I tried this and it did not stick", or "that works if you already have an audience".
What fixes it
They see it work. On somebody who looked like them at the start. Claims do not do this. A named client does.
What you hand them
Open one part of your method on one real client. What they tried before, what you changed, the number it moved. Call it One Client Breakdown. Not the whole method thinly, one part fully.
3They believe you but not that it applies to them
What stops them
Their size, their niche, their time. They say "not sure this works for someone at my size" or "my industry is different".
What fixes it
They see themselves in it. The last gap is almost never price. It is whether they can picture the result happening to them.
What you hand them
Your sales call already asks questions to decide if someone is right for you. Publish the questions as The Fit Check. Wrong-fit people leave for free. Right-fit people arrive already convinced.
→
All three get built. Only one gets promoted. They are all free to make, because they already exist. But you push one of them publicly this month and keep the other two for conversations. Push all three and people learn that you have three answers and no clear position.
